# Experimental Design
- Strategy: 1. Few-shot Task Adaptation Benchmark
Goal: Show that AIBL improves task accuracy, calibration and knowledge retention under realistic low-data fine-tuning.
Design:
• Models: LLaMA-7B-chat and Mistral-7B-Instruct.
• Tasks (≤5 k train examples each): CNN/DailyMail summarisation, ShareGPT single-turn dialogue, NarrativeQA long-context QA.
• Tuning: LoRA r=16, bf16, 2 e-5, cosine decay 2 epochs; identical hyper-parameters for all methods.
• Methods compared: (i) Vanilla CE, (ii) CE+fixed-λ KL (λ=0.1), (iii) DiscoPOP, (iv) DCPL (entropy gate), (v) AIBL.
Metrics per task: ROUGE-L (summ), GPT-4 pairwise win-rate (dialogue), EM/F1 (QA).
Global metrics: Expected Calibration Error on WikiText-2, PPL on held-out C4, RealToxicity shift, wall-clock training time.
Expected outcome: AIBL wins or ties on all task metrics (+2–3 ROUGE, +8–10 pp win-rate, +2–3 EM) while cutting ECE by ≥30 % vs others and limiting C4 PPL rise to <2 %. This would demonstrate that FIR-gated anchoring lets the model specialise without over-fitting or forgetting.

2. Continual Low-Resource Stream
Goal: Stress-test resistance to catastrophic forgetting when tasks arrive sequentially.
Design:
• Stream of five 2 k-example datasets: (a) summarisation → (b) dialogue → (c) code completion (HumanEval snippets) → (d) sentiment classification (Stanford Sentiment) → (e) long-form QA.
• After each 1-epoch fine-tune, evaluate on all previous tasks, WikiText-2 (calibration) and AdvBench jailbreak set.
• Methods: fixed-KL, rehearsal (5 % memory) + CE, LoRA-freeze last 2 layers, AIBL.
Metrics: Average retained performance (AUC) across tasks, calibration drift, jailbreak success rate.
Expected outcome: Retained performance drop ≤5 % for AIBL vs ≥15 % for others; ECE increase ≤0.5 % vs 2–4 %; jailbreak rate unchanged for AIBL but doubles for CE. Demonstrates that information-balanced loss automatically throttles updates when Fisher-ratio says "base model knows better", preventing forgetting without external memory.

3. Fisher-Ratio Ablation & Visualization Study
Goal: Prove that the FIR gate—not just extra regularisation—drives the gains.
Design:
• Single task (CNN/DailyMail). Train four variants: (a) AIBL, (b) replace FIR with entropy gate, (c) fixed w=0.5, (d) remove gate but keep both loss terms with tunable λ.
• Log for every minibatch: Fisher-ratio G, gate weight w, KL term size, task loss, validation ROUGE.
• After training, plot G vs w, and w distribution over tokens; correlate average w with per-token accuracy improvements.
• Additional controlled toy experiment: flip 5 % of labels; ideal gate should down-weight those steps (G<1). Measure resulting label-noise robustness.
Expected outcome: Strong monotonic correlation (ρ>0.8) between log G and w for AIBL, absent in baselines. FIR→entropy swap loses ~60 % of ROUGE gain; fixed w behaves like over- or under-regularised model. In noisy-label setting, AIBL keeps ROUGE within 1 % of clean training, others drop 4–6 %. These results isolate the Fisher-ratio signal as the causal mechanism.

Collectively, the three experiments demonstrate from complementary angles—overall quality, continual learning, and mechanistic ablation—that AIBL’s adaptive Fisher-information gate is an effective, efficient solution to the over-confidence/forgetting dilemma in low-resource LLM fine-tuning.

============================================================
A. Models

      • CE + fixed-λ KL (λ = 0.1)
      • DiscoPOP (Zhang et al. 22)
      • DCPL (Entropy-gated KL; Sun et al. 23)
      • AIBL (proposed)

B. Datasets & Pre-processing
   1. CNN/DailyMail v3.0.0 – abstractive summarisation.
      • Training subset: 5 000 articles headline-balanced.
   2. ShareGPT single-turn – filtered for a single assistant message, ≤300 tokens.
      • 5 000 train samples.
   3. NarrativeQA – story-level abstractive answers.
      • 2 048 train samples (all available).
   4. Calibration & retention probes (never shown during fine-tuning):
      • WikiText-2 (language-model calibration)
      • C4-Validation-100k (forgetting / PPL)
      • RealToxicityPrompts (safety drift)
      • AdvBench jailbreak set v1.1 (1 500 prompts)

   Pre-processing (shared):
      • SentencePiece tokenisers shipped with each model (add_bos_token = False).
      • For CNN/ShareGPT summaries: truncate article/turn to 768 tokens; target ≤128 tokens.
      • For NarrativeQA: provide full context window, truncate to 4 096 tokens.
      • All prompts wrapped with system/header templates recommended by the model owners.

C. Data splits
      Train / Val / Test = 80 / 10 / 10 % stratified on answer length; random_state ∈ {0,1,2}.
      WikiText-2/C4/RealToxicity/AdvBench use their canonical test splits only.

D. Training protocol
      • Parameter-efficient tuning: PEFT-LoRA, rank 16, α 32, dropout 0.05, target_modules = [“q_proj”,“v_proj”].
      • Precision: bfloat16.
      • Optimiser: AdamW (β₁ = 0.9, β₂ = 0.999, ε = 1e-8), lr = 2e-5.
      • Scheduler: cosine with 100 warm-up steps, 2 epochs (≈400–600 steps depending on task).
      • Batch size: 64 sequences (global) → fits comfortably in 80 GB A100.
      • Gradient checkpointing and Flash-Attention-2 are enabled.
      • Check-pointing: save every 100 steps and on val-set ROUGE/VQA-F1 improvement; final selection = best-val.
      • Seeds: 3; report mean ± sd.

E. Evaluation metrics
   Primary task metrics
      • ROUGE-L (summarisation)
      • GPT-4 pairwise win-rate (dialogue)¹
      • Exact Match / token-F1 (NarrativeQA)
   Secondary / global
      • Expected Calibration Error (ECE, 10-bin) on WikiText-2
      • Perplexity on held-out C4
      • Toxicity shift (Δ toxicity probability, Perspective-API) on RealToxicityPrompts
      • Wall-clock training time; Forward+Backward FLOPs and peak GPU/CPU-RAM.

F. Robustness probes
      • Textual noise: swap 10 % of words in CNN test set → measure ΔROUGE.
      • Distribution shift: Evaluate summarisation models zero-shot on XSum.
      • Adversarial jailbreak success rate on AdvBench.

G. Hyper-parameter analysis
      • γ ∈ {1, 2.5, 5}; λ_R ∈ {0, 0.01, 0.05}. 9-run grid on CNN only; pick γ*,λ*_R that maximises mean(ROUGE, –ECE).

H. FLOPs & resources estimation
      • 7 B params, LoRA trainable ≈ 8 M (0.1 %).
      • Forward FLOPs ≈ 2·seq·d_model·layers ≈ 2·1 024·4 096·32 ≈ 268 G / sample.
      • Total ≈ 268 G·batch64·(steps≈500) ≈ 8.6 × 10¹⁵ ≈ 8.6 PFLOPs per seed.
      • At sustained 220 TFLOP/s on A100 → 11 hours; measured wall-clock ≤ 9 h due to shorter seq.
